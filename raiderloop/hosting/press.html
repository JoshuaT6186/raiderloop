Press kit

In one line

Flyer is a notebook-style campus app that puts classes, events, friends, food, maps and deadlines in one place for college students, starting at Texas Tech.

Made by

Joshua, a Texas Tech student, under Eternity Works LLC. Independent and student-built.

Platforms

iPhone (in App Store review) and the web at this site's /app.

About Flyer

Campus life is scattered across dozens of websites and apps. Flyer brings it into one place: today's classes with walk times, events and clubs, a map of every building with what's open now, a planner that reads your syllabus, friends and group spaces, and an AI assistant called Pilot for campus questions.

Flyer looks like a student's notebook, with sticky notes, tape and folder tabs, and it's built around privacy: schedules and grades stay on the phone, photos are friends-only and checked before anyone sees them, and posts disappear after 24 hours unless saved.

Standout features include Flyer Drop (send files and notes phone to phone, or to your own laptop, with a paper-plane animation), Present Mode (pair with any TV or class PC by code), Flight Score (points for exploring campus and meeting up), and a public Campus Guide for anyone curious about Texas Tech.

The story

Flyer began as RaiderLoop, a practice project to fix Texas Tech's disconnected student tools. Student Government liked it and pitched it to the university. When the official route stalled, the founder published it independently, redesigned it as Flyer, and kept going.
